Every few months someone in my class says the same thing: *this country needs one good leader.* One person who will fix the roads, the schools, the courts. We nod, and then we go back to our phones.

I used to believe it too. But the more time I spent in Hope's reading circles, the more I noticed what that sentence quietly does. It hands our responsibility to someone who hasn't arrived yet.

## Justice is a habit

Our manifesto describes justice not as control through force, but as an inner strength. In practice, that looks small: sharing notes with the student who missed class, refusing to cheat, standing up when someone is mocked for their accent.

> Only a just individual can contribute to the creation of a just society.

## Awareness is action

Knowing things is easy now. Acting on them is the hard part. Awareness, as we use the word, means turning what you know into what you do, and thinking for yourself instead of repeating what's loudest.

So here is my suggestion. Stop waiting. Start an embassy, run a Hope Circle, write the essay you keep talking about. The saviour we're waiting for is a few thousand of us, doing ordinary things well.
